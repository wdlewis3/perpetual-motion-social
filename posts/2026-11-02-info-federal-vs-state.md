## Caption (paste-ready)

When a state creates a psilocybin program, the headline can make it sound like the question is settled. It is a little more layered than that.

A state program is a set of rules a state writes for how something may be offered inside that state. Federal approval is a separate process: the FDA reviews trial data and decides whether a drug can be marketed as a medicine. Psilocybin and MDMA are still in clinical trials, and neither has completed that federal review. How state programs and federal law fit together is a legal question that can change, so read each headline for which one it is about.

For veterans and families, that distinction matters when deciding what is actually available today and what is still research. Stigma tends to blur it, because people assume it is either already everywhere or never going to happen.

Educational only. Not medical or legal advice. Laws vary by state; talk to a licensed clinician. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 (press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line).

Which of these two have you seen mixed up in the news?
